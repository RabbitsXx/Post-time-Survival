# Hooks: keeping viewers past the first seconds (study, 2026-11-01)

## Evidence and how much to trust it
- Our own channel: Pig War v3 "stayed to watch" 66.2 %, average viewed 82.9 % (YouTube Studio AI summary, first day). Views/likes per clip: tickle 1,270 / 30, Pig War 1,476 / 24 (vidIQ CSV 2026-11-01).
- Reference clips (vidIQ scene walkthroughs of 7 Shorts, n = 7): what happens in the first 3 s is listed below. Strongest evidence we have, but a small sample and vidIQ's own summary, not frame-by-frame viewing.
- Web search (creator-tool blogs; no peer-reviewed study found): most drop-off happens in the first seconds; curiosity gap + pattern interrupt are the usual advice; benchmarks conflict (stayed-to-watch "good" = 65 %, 70 %+, 75-80 %). YouTube publishes no official threshold. Treat numbers as channel-relative: compare our own clips, same format and window.
- Metric definitions: *Viewed vs swiped away* = share of feed impressions that did not swipe away; average percentage viewed counts only people who stayed. So a hook problem shows up in "stayed to watch", not in average viewed.

## Rules distilled
1. Frame 0 is already mid-action or mid-weird. No setup shot, no standing around.
2. Sentence 1 = the strangest outcome; the picture shows that same thing at the same moment.
3. A pattern interrupt within ~1.5 s (hit, splash, bang, whip, sudden pop) so the swipe thumb stops.
4. Open a contradiction or a stake ("nothing happens ... you fall apart").
5. Close-up, one hero, big. Labels point at the weird thing and never cover it (or the face).
6. Start speaking at ~0.1 s and keep ~3.5 words/s through the hook.
7. Do not spend the first 3 s on a card, a title, or the series name.

## What to change in the next clip (Pastry War) and how to measure
- Open on the action: the pastry shop being smashed / a cake hitting a face / a cannon blast, with sentence 1 saying it ("A bakery started a war.").
- A sound hit and a visual jerk in the first 1.5 s; label with an arrow on the odd object.
- Keep the cover as the first 4 frames only if it is also an action frame.
- Measure per clip: stayed-to-watch % (YouTube Studio) and average % viewed; compare clips of the same length and lane (tickle, octopus, wrinkle are science/body; Pig War and Pastry War are history).
