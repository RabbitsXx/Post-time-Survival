# Why Your Fingers Wrinkle — source note
Checked 2026-11-01 by web search (summaries only; primary papers were not opened and the session cannot open most pages).
- "Your fingers don't wrinkle because they soak up water. Your nervous system does it on purpose." — the vasoconstriction explanation (Wilder-Smith & Chow, 2003 summary); the old osmosis explanation is treated as at most minor. Sources seen in search: Mapping Ignorance, ScienceABC, The Scientist, HowEverythingWorks.
- "It squeezes the blood vessels under your skin, and the skin sinks into wrinkles." — same mechanism, simplified. How exactly water triggers it is still unclear (Wilder-Smith).
- "Fingers with nerve damage don't wrinkle at all." — observation going back to Pickering & Lewis (1936, polio nerve damage); cutting sympathetic nerve fibres stops wrinkling. Phrased as a general observation; wording "at all" may be too strong for every case: soften if a source disagrees.
- "Some scientists think the wrinkles work like tire treads" — rain-tread hypothesis (Changizi, 2011). Framed as "think".
- "Other studies found no grip benefit. Science is still arguing." — results are mixed: some studies report better grip on wet objects (e.g. a 2021 PLOS ONE study, 516 people), another (2014, 40 volunteers) found no dexterity advantage. Exact citations were not verified (Kareklas 2013 / Haseleu 2015 could not be confirmed by search).
- Jokes, not claims: raisin face, bulging eyes, marbles both slipping, "Then we invented soap."
