# An Octopus Has Three Hearts — source note
Checked 2026-11-01 by web search (snippets only; the pages themselves could not be opened from the session, and no primary paper was read).
- "An octopus has three hearts." — consistent across many sources; two branchial (gill) hearts and one systemic (body) heart.
- "when it swims, one of them stops" / "Swimming pauses that body heart" — widely repeated (e.g. Live Science, quoting researcher Onthank: swimming puts such high pressure on the heart that it may be better to stop it for a few moments). A Science Blog piece argues the popular telling overstates it: the pause is a measured, brief interruption, not a switch-off for the whole swim. The script says "pauses" and "stops" in the hook only as shorthand; keep the description wording careful.
- "so it wears the octopus out fast" / "Scientists think that's why octopuses would rather crawl" — framed as a hypothesis in the sources ("suggests"); the script says "scientists think".
- "Their blood is blue, too, because it runs on copper instead of iron" — hemocyanin (copper-based) instead of hemoglobin (iron-based); multiple sources.
- Visual gags: Poly's "...Same." and "Octopuses hate cardio." are jokes, not claims. The heart icons are diagram symbols, not anatomy.
