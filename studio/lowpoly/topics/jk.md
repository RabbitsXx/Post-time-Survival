# The War Named After A Sailor's Ear (War of Jenkins' Ear) — source note
Fact-checked 2026-11-01 against the raw text of the Wikipedia article "War of Jenkins' Ear" (opened via curl). Encyclopedia source, not scholarly. Other pages (History.com etc.) could not be opened from the session.

| script line | status | source / note |
|---|---|---|
| "This war is named after a sailor's ear." | OK | "The name derives from Robert Jenkins, a British sea captain whose ear was allegedly severed in April 1731..." Not claimed: that the ear alone caused the war (the article stresses trade and colonial disputes). |
| "In 1731, Spanish coast guards searched the British ship Rebecca for smuggled goods. Captain Robert Jenkins later claimed they cut off his ear." | OK | brig Rebecca carrying smuggled sugar; Jenkins "later alleging his ear was partially amputated during the search by Spanish customs officers". The hook shot dramatises his claim ("allegedly" is also the last word of the clip). |
| "Seven years later, British politicians showed him off in Parliament to build support for war." | OK | 1738: opposition (Tory) politicians "exhibited Jenkins in the House of Commons" in their campaign against Walpole. (Whether he showed an ear in a jar is NOT in the article: not claimed.) |
| "Britain went to war with Spain in 1739." | OK | war 1739-1748; George II declared war on 15 June (1739); Parliament ratified 23 Oct. |
| "Its biggest battle was a failed British attack on Cartagena, with heavy losses." | OK | "The most significant operation of the war was a failed British attack on Cartagena in 1741, which resulted in heavy casualties and ship losses". |
| "When it ended in 1748, Britain had mostly failed to get what it wanted." | OK | Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle; "Britain largely failed to achieve its original territorial and economic ambitions in the Americas". |
| "Today, it is still marked every May in Savannah, Georgia." | OK | "commemorated annually on the last Saturday in May at Wormsloe Plantation in Savannah, Georgia". The banner "JENKINS' EAR DAY" and the ear hat are our dramatisation. |
| "A nine year war, named after one ear. Allegedly." | OK / joke | 1739 to 1748 = nine years; "allegedly" as in the article. |

Visual simplifications: uniforms, ships, the fort and the map are stylised; the map shows only four places (Britain, Spain, the Colombian coast around Cartagena, Georgia) and is not to scale; the Spanish flag is drawn as a red saltire on white (the Cross of Burgundy). Giant wigs and the ear monument are jokes.
