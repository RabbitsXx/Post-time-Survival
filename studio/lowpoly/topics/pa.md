# A Bakery Started A War (The Pastry War) — source note
Checked 2026-11-01 by web search (summaries; the pages themselves could not be opened from the session; secondary/popular histories, not scholarly sources).
- "A bakery started a war between France and Mexico." — the Pastry War (Nov 1838 - Mar 1839); sources: Wikipedia (Pastry War, Battle of Veracruz 1838), History.com, Mexico News Daily, britishempire.co.uk.
- "Mexican officers trashed a French pastry shop, and the chef billed them sixty thousand pesos." — French pastry chef Remontel, shop in Tacubaya, looted by Mexican army officers; he claimed 60,000 pesos. The YEAR of the looting is given as 1828 by some sources and 1832 by others, so the script does not name a year.
- "France demanded six hundred thousand, blockaded Mexico's ports, and bombarded Veracruz." — France demanded 600,000 pesos (the demand included more claims than the pastry shop); blockade of Gulf ports from 1838; bombardment of San Juan de Ulua / Veracruz 27 Nov - 5 Dec 1838.
- "Mexico's General Santa Anna lost a leg to French cannon fire." — leg lost at Veracruz in 1838 (amputated below the knee; a French cannonball/grapeshot; some sources place the amputation early 1839).
- "Britain stepped in, and Mexico agreed to pay." — British mediation; Mexico agreed to pay the 600,000 pesos; French forces left on 9 March 1839.
- "Years later, Santa Anna gave that leg a state funeral." — 1842: the leg was exhumed, paraded to Mexico City and buried with ceremony and cannon salutes (several sources; one gives 27 Sep 1842, Santa Paula cemetery). In 1844 rioters dug it up and dragged it through the streets. His prosthetic leg was captured by US forces at Cerro Gordo (1847) and is displayed in the Illinois State Military Museum.
- Joke, not a claim: "The leg got a funeral. The cakes got nothing."
- Visual simplifications (not claims): uniforms, ships, fort and set dressing are stylised; Santa Anna is shown with a peg leg from the start of the shot where he loses the leg.
