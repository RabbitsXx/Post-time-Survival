# The War Named After A Pastry Shop (Pastry War) — source note
Fact-checked 2026-11-01 against the raw text of Wikipedia articles (opened via curl): "Pastry War", "Battle of Veracruz (1838)", "Antonio López de Santa Anna". These are secondary/encyclopedia sources, not scholarly ones. History.com, Mexico News Daily, Texas State Library pages could not be opened from the session.

| script line | status | source / note |
|---|---|---|
| "This war is named after a pastry shop." | OK | Pastry War: war "named after" the pastry chef; France acted over several complaints, the chef was one. NOT claimed: that a bakery alone started the war. |
| "A French pastry chef complained that Mexican officers looted his shop, and demanded sixty thousand pesos." | OK, as a claim | Remontel's complaint to Louis-Philippe: officers looted his shop in 1832 (Wikipedia), demanded 60,000 pesos (shop valued under 1,000). Mexican sources say the officers simply refused to pay their bills: the opening shot dramatises HIS version. Year not spoken (other sources say 1828). |
| "France then demanded six hundred thousand pesos" | OK | 1838, PM Molé; 600,000 pesos (3 million francs), covering many complaints, not only the shop. |
| "blockaded Mexico's ports" | OK | Gulf ports from Yucatán to the Rio Grande, from Nov 1838. |
| "bombarded the fortress at Veracruz" | OK | San Juan de Ulúa citadel bombarded 27-28 Nov 1838 (Battle of Veracruz). |
| "General Santa Anna was hit by French cannon fire and lost a leg." | OK | Santa Anna article: injuries to left leg and hand by cannon fire, leg amputated (6 Dec 1838 per Battle of Veracruz; war article says grapeshot). |
| "Britain brokered peace, and Mexico agreed to pay. Mexico never did." | OK, with a caveat | Pastry War: British-brokered peace, treaty 9 Mar 1839, Mexico agreed to pay 600,000 pesos; "The damages were never paid" (also used to justify the 1861 French intervention). The same article says France took trade promises in place of indemnities: wording kept simple. |
| "Santa Anna buried his leg with full military honors." | OK | Santa Anna article and Battle of Veracruz: leg buried with full military honors. |
| "Six years later, a mob dug it up and dragged it through the streets." | OK | Santa Anna article: he fled in Dec 1844; the buried leg in the capital was dug up by a mob and dragged through the streets. |
| "The leg got a funeral. The cakes got nothing." | joke | not a claim |

Removed after checking: the "1842 state funeral with a parade" (seen only in search summaries of sources that could not be opened; not in the Wikipedia articles) and the original hook "A bakery started a war" (overclaim).
Visual simplifications: uniforms/ships/fort are stylised; Santa Anna is drawn with his peg leg from the start of the shot where he loses the leg; he lost his LEFT leg and the model has the peg on the character's left; the mob is generic soldiers.
