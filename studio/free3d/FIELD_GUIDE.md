# Field guide: how good short animation is made (research notes, 2026-10-31)

Honest scope: first-hand breakdowns of the big channels (Low Poly Shorts, Public Nonsense) were NOT found. What follows comes from sources that could be read; each point says where it comes from. Third-party blogs are weaker than interviews.

## What the evidence says
1. **One author, one hand.** Skibidi Toilet was made by one person who did animation, direction, writing and editing himself in Source Filmmaker (Cartoon Brew interview, quoted by Wikipedia's article and search summaries). Wikipedia: first episode was 11 seconds; early uploads were frequent (algorithm favours frequent uploaders) and later spacing grew "to improve quality". Cartoon Brew credits him with understanding pacing, camerawork, sound design and storytelling.
   -> For us: a recognisable look and a person's judgement beat volume. Slow down as soon as the idea works.
2. **The 12 principles of animation (Disney, Johnston and Thomas; Wikipedia).** Most relevant to short comedy:
   - *Anticipation*: show the character prepare (look off-screen, wind-up) before the action lands.
   - *Staging*: present the idea so it is "completely and unmistakably clear" (framing, silhouette, one focus per shot).
   - *Squash and stretch / exaggeration*: weight and flexibility; extremes read as funny.
   - *Follow-through / overlapping action*: parts keep moving after the body stops.
   - *Slow in, slow out*: ease into and out of moves.
   - *Moving hold*: even a still character breathes or shifts weight so it never looks lifeless. (Added to our renderer: stronger breathing + slow weight shift on idle.)
3. **30 s comedy template** (commercial-script writer, Amazing Audio Ads, via search summary): ~12 s setup and escalation, ~3 s turn, ~5 s closing button. Cut every setup word that does not build to the payoff. Misdirection works because the audience fills the gap with its own assumption (Misdirection in Comedy, creativehumanities.github.io). General comedy craft, not animation specific.
4. **Briefs for low-poly channels** (Freelancer listings): blocky characters with a reusable expression sheet, bold silhouettes, exaggerated motion arcs, snappy comedic timing, "no dead air in a 24 s video", music and SFX added last.
5. **Shorts retention**: third-party sources agree the opening decides swipe-away but disagree on numbers; no official source was found. Compare against your own Shorts.

## Rules we adopt
- One idea, one reason to exist per clip; no label-swap variations. Fewer clips, finished properly.
- Write the micro-storyboard (6 shots max) before rendering; first 2 seconds must show the situation.
- Setup short, one turn, a button line at the end. Cut dead air (our shots had long minimum holds and padding; now tighter).
- Acting: anticipation before reactions, exaggerated expressions, never a dead-still character.
- Variety must be real: new people, new place, new structure (done: Nana, Bo, outside the store).
- Synthetic voices are a tell for many viewers: prefer fewer lines, or ask the owner for a human voice.
- QC every shot; no meaningless labels on covers.
