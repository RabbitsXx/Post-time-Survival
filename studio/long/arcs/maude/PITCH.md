# PITCH: GRANDMA MAUDE'S LAST STAND

**Share line (<= 20 words):** Grandma Maude is 82, alone, deaf in one ear, and she brought her javelin. The zombies picked the wrong bungalow.
**WHAT IF:** a retired javelin champion who lives alone has to defend her home, cross the neighbourhood on a mobility scooter and rescue her old friends, with only a teenage grandson on the phone.
**Audience feeling map:** Part 1: amusement > dread > cheer (she is awesome) > worry. Part 2: thrill > laugh > fear > awe. Part 3: tension > warmth > big cheer > tears of laughter.
**Hero:** MAUDE (82, ex-Olympic javelin thrower from 1964, widow, lives with her pug Biscuit). WANT: get the box of her late husband's heart pills from the pharmacy and bring her friends home. FLAW: she is deaf in the left ear and mishears everything her grandson says. Voice: dry, unbothered ("Oh, for heaven's sake."). SIGNATURE FIGHT: the javelin (a throw is one kill, then she must go and fetch it) + the rollator as a battering ram + whatever is on the kitchen counter. CATCHPHRASE: "I've had worse Tuesdays."
**Other characters (max 3):** THEO (14, her grandson, hiding in a locked flat across town, heard only as a phone voice in a video-call card with his face lit by a monitor; he gives video-game advice that is always slightly wrong). BISCUIT (the pug, silent, wears a tiny red cape, barks at the right moment). MR. PATEL (80, pharmacist, appears only in part 3, locked in his shop, a calm retired boxer).
**Antagonist / threat:** the dead of Maple Close are neighbours in their gardening clothes and slippers; they are slow in the morning and fast after dark (a rule the viewer learns in part 1), and they follow the smell of the baking Maude leaves on windowsills.
**World:** (1) the bungalow: kitchen, hallway with a stairlift, back garden with a shed (warm yellow, doilies, clutter); (2) Maple Close street at golden hour (pastel houses, hedges, a stray inflatable snowman); (3) the pharmacy (green cross, mint tiles); (4) the garden centre (jungle of plants, fountain); (5) the care home Sunny Pines (bright pink corridors, bingo hall). Each has one memorable object: the stairlift, the inflatable snowman, a giant mortar and pestle sign, a garden gnome army, a bingo cage.
**Running gag (three beats):** Theo says "Hit it in the head!"; Maude mishears (set-up: "Eat a bread?" she eats toast and hits it with the toast rack); repeat (a second mishear: "Use the ladder!" -> "Use the Latte!"; she throws her coffee); twist (the third time she hears him perfectly and does the opposite on purpose: "I heard you, dear. I'm choosing violence.").
**Real survival tip and where it WINS the story:** the noise alarm and the one-door fortress: tin cans on a string across the doorways warn you before anyone arrives, and a home with only ONE usable door can be defended by one person. In part 3 the care home siege is won because Maude seals every door but one, strings cans at the last door, and her friends fight in the corridor she controls; the cans wake the whole building at the right moment and decide the climax.
**Part 1 (THE KITCHEN, 55-65 s):**
- poster moment: Maude in her housecoat holding a javelin in the hallway as a silhouette hand rattles the front door glass.
- poster moment: Maude throws the javelin through the kitchen window and it pins a zombie's gardening hat to the shed; she goes out in slippers to retrieve it.
- poster moment: the stairlift as a drawbridge (she rides it down with a casserole).
- She is ALONE in the bungalow all part (solo set piece: Maude vs the first three zombies, with Biscuit); Theo only on the phone.
- fight 1: javelin through the window (THWACK!) then she must fetch it; fight 2: casserole dish bonk at the door (BONK!); fight 3: rollator battering ram in the hallway (CLANG!); fight 4: hose and garden shears duel in the back garden (SNIP!); fight 5: toast rack vs a zombie in the kitchen (the mishear gag set-up).
- reversal: the dead are slow in the day and she relaxes; the light goes (sunset) and they speed up.
- tip: she strings tin cans on a thread across the back door; the cans rattle at night and save her.
- END QUESTION: the pharmacy is across the neighbourhood and her scooter has half a battery; will she make it before the dark? Open of part 2: the answer is "yes, barely", plus a surprise: Biscuit is on the scooter basket.
**Part 2 (THE SCOOTER, 55-65 s):**
- poster moment: Maude on a mobility scooter in a golden-hour street with a pug in a red cape, a zombie horde in the rearview.
- poster moment: she throws the javelin from the scooter at full speed and it takes out a zombie on a hedge (the best shot of the part).
- poster moment: the inflatable snowman falling on the horde.
- SOLO set piece: Maude alone on the scooter for the whole part (chase through Maple Close as a 2.5D lane), Theo as a voice.
- fight 1: javelin throw while driving; fight 2: she drives the scooter over a zombie lawn gnome army; fight 3: the scooter basket bag of oranges as grenades; fight 4: Biscuit's bark makes a zombie drop; fight 5: she faces the pharmacist's locked door with the rollator and a zombie is behind her (CLANG!).
- reversal: the scooter dies; she must push it uphill; she uses the scooter's battery as a defibrillator joke ("Not today, dear.").
- Mr. Patel, behind glass, holds up a sign: "FRIENDS AT SUNNY PINES ARE TRAPPED."
- END QUESTION: she has the pills, but the call from Sunny Pines is her old friend Dot asking for help; will she leave the pills? Open of part 3: she chooses the friends, and a surprise: Theo is alive and is outside the care home.
**Part 3 (THE LAST STAND, 55-65 s):**
- poster moment: Maude at the entrance of Sunny Pines, javelin on her shoulder, ten old friends behind her with canes and a bingo cage as a weapon.
- poster moment: the bingo cage rolling into the corridor of zombies like a bowling ball.
- poster moment: Maude's last javelin throw down a long pink corridor.
- Alone at first: Maude clears the front hall alone (solo set piece), then reunites with Dot and the friends only for the final stand.
- fight 1: javelin down the corridor; fight 2: bingo balls on the floor (zombies slip); fight 3: rollator charge with a friend on it; fight 4: cane duel with Dot; fight 5: the tin-can alarm wakes the building and the corridor fills; the one open door becomes the trap.
- reversal: the cans rattle too early and the siege is on, then Maude realises it was the plan (she says "I heard you, dear").
- tip wins: the cans on the one open door give the friends a ten-second warning and the corridor trap works.
- END QUESTION (series question): Theo walks in and says "Gran, I could not stay." Will the dead ever leave Maple Close? Next series teaser: a new hero.
**Music idea:** D minor swing with a jazzy upright bass for comedy, a flute theme for Maude ("The Javelin Waltz"), brass stabs in the chases, a bingo-hall organ for the final stand. **Sound hook:** the javelin whistle then a wet THWACK, followed by Maude's "Oh, for heaven's sake."
**Title + thumbnail idea:** "Grandma Maude vs 50 Zombies" (<= 60 chars); thumbnail: Maude in a housecoat and slippers, javelin raised, pug in a cape, horde as silhouettes, golden light.

**Why this beats the runners-up:** every part has a lovable hero doing something only she could do, the fights are the joke and the thrill at once, and she is alone nearly the whole time, so the cast is small and cheap to draw. THE LAST FERRY stays in the bank because it is a team story and the studio just made two ensemble topics.
